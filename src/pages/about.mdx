**Matteo Cavallo** is a Senior Web Engineer at [Qonto](https://qonto.com), based in Paris. He crafts meaningful digital experiences with care, clarity and intention — helping purpose-driven people leave things better than they found them.

* * *

I'm also someone who reads Marcus Aurelius on Sunday mornings, who picks up the guitar when the day needs a different kind of thinking, who photographs the light in Paris with a small camera and no particular agenda.

I've spent a long time wondering how those two things fit together — the engineer and the humanist, the builder and the reader. Maybe they don't need to. Maybe the point is to carry both without pretending one doesn't exist.

This site is part of that answer. It's connected to D'ici, a newsletter I'm writing about technology, generational change, and what it means to be human in an era that keeps asking us to be faster.

I'm not in a hurry. Or I'm trying not to be.

## Vision

The compass for how I build, think, and live.

1. **Good work is a practice, not an achievement.** Work done with care — with the person on the other side genuinely in mind — is one of the few quiet acts that can leave the world slightly better than you found it. Not a standard to reach once. A discipline to return to every day.
2. **Clarity is a form of care.** Precision isn't coldness. Making something clear — a system, a sentence, an interface — is an act of respect for whoever receives it. Design that holds people without them noticing: that's the goal.
3. **Slowness is a choice, not a flaw.** I work in an industry that worships speed. I'm trying to resist it — not out of stubbornness, but because the best things I've made took longer than planned, and were better for it.
4. **Carry both.** The engineer and the humanist, the builder and the reader — I've stopped trying to resolve the tension between them. The point is to carry both without pretending one doesn't exist. Marcus Aurelius wrote philosophy between military campaigns. That's the tradition I want to be part of.
5. **Technology should serve human depth.** I'm building D'ici because I think the conversation about AI is missing something: what we do with our humanness when machines get good at everything else. I don't have the answer. But I think the question matters.
6. **Roots.** I'm from a small town in central Italy. I plan to go back. Knowing where you come from — and where you're returning to — shapes what you build and why.
