I don't know exactly when I started photographing thresholds.

Not streets, not buildings, not faces. Thresholds.

Arches that separate shadow from light. Paths that disappear beyond the hill. Figures from behind, walking toward something not yet visible.

I looked back at my recent photos the way you reread the pages of a diary you didn't know you were writing. There's a thread. Not of color, not of landscape; of posture. Always in balance. 

Always _nel mezzo_.

---

I've lived in Paris for a few years now. Long enough that Italy no longer feels entirely like home, not long enough to stop feeling like a stranger here. When I go back: my family house, the familiar faces, my niece growing, the restaurant where my mum works. I find myself looking at everything with a new distance. Not cold. Luminous, maybe. 

The way you look at something you love and are afraid of losing.

I shoot Italy in color. The wild Tuscany, the Ligurian sea, the June light on the hills. It's an almost superstitious act, as if color could preserve something that time is slowly shifting away.

I shoot Paris in black and white. Not a conscious aesthetic choice, at least not at first. I understand it only now: the present hasn't found its palette yet. It's still developing.

---

David Lynch said that to see the light you first need to gather enough darkness. I put it in the caption of a photo taken in Paris: a figure in backlight, standing on the exact border between the two. I didn't know it yet, but I was describing myself.

Darkness isn't sadness.

It's simply the space where you're still figuring out who you are.

---

There's an Italian word that doesn't translate well into any other language: _sospeso_. It doesn't just mean suspended in the air — it means waiting, unresolved, caught between two states. It's used for delayed trains, for verdicts not yet delivered, for people who haven't decided yet, [for coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caff%C3%A8_sospeso).

I was _sospeso_. In the most literal and heaviest sense of the word.

For years I carried something I hadn't chosen — a legal case that had fallen on me by mistake, a slow and indifferent machine that puts your life on hold while you try to move forward. You can't close it. You can't forget it. You can only keep going, with that thing hanging somewhere just out of reach.

In those years I built anyway. I moved countries. I learned to work in a language that wasn't mine, then in another. I wrote code, took photographs, played guitar, started writing words. Not because I was brave but because I didn't know what else to do. Because stopping would have meant letting that suspension become my identity.

But a part of me was waiting. 

Holding its breath.

---

A few days ago, it ended.

Not with fireworks, not with a cinematic liberation. With a document, a signature, the end of something that should never have begun. And then silence — strange, sudden, almost uncomfortable. Like when a background noise disappears and you realize you'd stopped hearing it.

Only then did I understand how much space it had been taking up. How much silent energy it had been absorbing, every day, without me fully noticing.

The photos I take now have a different color. Or maybe it's me who looks at them differently.

The turquoise of that sea isn't just a color — it's proof that color still exists, that light returns, that you can stop shooting in black and white. Not because the darkness was wrong. But because the darkness did its work.

_The more darkness you gather, the more light you can see._

I've come out of the _mezzo_. Or maybe not. Maybe the _mezzo_ has simply become a less heavy place to be. A place from which you can already see something in the distance that resembles where you want to go.

I don't know what it's called yet. But this time I'm not waiting for someone to tell me.

_Sospeso nel mezzo_.
