I'm reading these words from Paris. I wrote them before leaving — like a letter 
to myself, one of those gestures you make without quite knowing why.

Back then I thought I was looking for something, I'm not sure what, and I'm not 
sure I've found it. But the question, at least, has become more precise.



---

_September 2024_

_By the time you hear these words, I'll already be on a plane to Paris._

_Who knows what I'll be thinking. Maybe about the life I'm leaving behind, or the 
new one waiting for me. Maybe I'll be looking out the window, trying to figure out 
whether up there, between the clouds, you can actually see the future._

_I like to let my mind travel when I'm on a plane. I think about the people I've 
loved, the ones I still love, the memories I carry like an old backpack I never had 
the courage to put down. And then I imagine what's waiting for me when I land, as 
if it were a book still to be written._

_Life has been full so far. Not everything went smoothly, but all things considered, 
I'm here. Survived — perhaps a little bruised, but still whole._

---

A few days before leaving, I was talking with a friend. One of those people who 
always has both feet on the ground.

"You know Alessà, there's a thought that's been haunting me lately: why do we make 
life so complicated? What is it that drives us to always want to change, to improve, 
to never be satisfied?"

"It's capitalism mattè, modern society, all that bullshit. You know it better than I do."

"Maybe. But I don't think it's just that. It's like we have an invisible engine 
inside us, pushing us further even when we're exhausted."

"Look at animals. They don't wake up one day saying: I'm done, I'm dropping 
everything and moving to Paris. They just live. But us? We always need something 
more. Maybe it's evolution. Maybe it's a curse."

That evening I rewatched [The Holdovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holdovers) 
— a film about those who stay, who get left behind while the world moves on, and 
discover something precise only because they didn't move.

It made me think. I'm not immune to that restlessness, that need to be more, to do 
more. But that night, instead of fighting it, I sat with it. I thought about the 
backpack of memories — the people left behind, the ones I still love, all the things 
left unsaid.

---

People are divided into two types: Pirates and Gardeners. Pirates sail, explore, 
search. Gardeners stop, cultivate, wait.

For a long time, I thought I had to choose.

But the Greeks had a word for what comes after: [νόστος, nostos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostos) 
— the return home. That's where *nostalgia* comes from: not a longing for the past, 
but the physical ache of someone who hasn't arrived yet.

Ulysses was the Pirate above all others. Twenty years of war and open sea, of 
monsters and enchanted islands. And yet that entire journey was just the longest 
way back to Ithaca. To his garden.

It took me a while to understand: Pirates and Gardeners are not two types of people. 
They're the same person, in different phases.

---

I'm writing these words from my studio, in Paris. Next to me, on the windowsill, 
there's a small garden: basil, thyme, parsley, rosemary.

Every Pirate, in the end, is just looking for a way back to their garden.
